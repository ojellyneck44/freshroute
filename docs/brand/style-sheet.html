FreshRoute · Stevie M. · Internship case study19 Nov 2026 · 1

One promise, kept in the open.

When a driver starts a route, FreshRoute locks an arrival time: the promise. Everything in the brand comes back to whether that promise is being kept. The ring shows it, the colour reports it, and the footage backs it up. Cody's problem was never a lack of maps. It was "oh, the truck's here" while he was in the middle of something else, and not knowing how long the yogurt had been out of the fridge.

The principle everything else serves

Honest time

Every number has a baseline: "6 min behind your 10:13 promise", never just "late". We never round a late arrival into an on-time one.

Calm at speed

Drivers look at the iPad for under a second at a time. While the van moves, the screen gets quieter: bigger type, fewer words, no decorative motion.

Evidence, not surveillance

Footage and motion markers protect the driver as much as the food. The tone is that of a dashcam that has your back, not a manager looking over your shoulder.

Native, not novelty

It should feel like it shipped with the iPad: system type for UI, Liquid Glass only on things that float, and Apple's own conventions for sheets, lists and controls.

Personality, in one line

A calm dispatcher who has done this route a hundred times: dry, precise, on your side. Not a cheerleader, not a cop.

What we leave out

Gamification (cut by the design studio vote), confetti, streaks, mascots and stock photos of smiling volunteers. The personality comes from the type, the leaf and the ring's motion.

Night road, one green.

The neutrals are a slightly green-tinted asphalt, so the dark mode feels like a road at night rather than a generic grey. There is one brand colour, Sprout, and it means "on time". Apricot means "needs a look", Tomato means a broken promise or something destructive, and Frost is only ever the cold chain.

Why green is the brand colour

The design studio voted "colour creates urgency" #1. If the brand colour is the on-time colour, a screen that looks most like FreshRoute is a screen where everything is fine.

Food names, on purpose

Sprout, Apricot, Tomato and Frost come from what's in the van. They are easy to say in a design review and they're a small bit of personality that costs nothing.

No gradients in the UI

The only gradient in the product is inside the app icon. Interface colour is flat, with one exception: glass picks up whatever is behind it.

Dark by default. Daylight when the sun is on the glass.

You asked for dark mode, and it stays the default. But a dark iPad on a dashboard at 2 p.m. in Denton mirrors the windshield and becomes hard to read. That's why Google Maps and CarPlay switch by time of day. Safari can't read the light sensor, so FreshRoute does the same: dark by default, Daylight in the driving screens between sunrise and sunset, with a manual override. Signage, interface, record.

Headlines, screen titles, store names and the wordmark. Overpass is based on Highway Gothic, the alphabet on US road signs, which was drawn to be read at speed from a distance. A route app set in road-sign type.

Open Font License, self-hosted as a variable woff2 so the PWA works offline. Used at 22 px and up, weights 700–800. Its middle dot sits tight, so separators get extra space.

Every control, list, label and live number. On iPad, iPhone and Mac this is SF Pro, so controls feel native, render crisply and cost zero bytes. Windows gets Segoe UI, Android gets Roboto.

The ticking rule

Anything that changes while you watch (ETA, cold timer, countdown, speed) is set in the system face with tabular-nums, so digits don't jitter sideways. Overpass is only for numbers that sit still, such as a report headline.

Glass is for things that float.

Glass

Floating controls over the map or video: maneuver card, ETA ring, camera window, toolbar, sheet grabber, tab bar. Material: 56% Asphalt 850, 24 px blur, 170% saturation, 1 px white 16% edge, 24% top highlight. Over busy map areas (behind the bottom tray) the tint rises to 80%.

Never glass

Content: lists, tables, reports, forms, cards stacked on solid backgrounds. Glass on glass. Anything with more than two lines of body text. Those use solid Asphalt 900/850, like this panel.

Fallbacks

prefers-reduced-transparency and Low Power Mode switch glass to solid Asphalt 850 at 96%. Over bright map areas (Daylight), the tint rises to 72% so text keeps 4.5:1.

FreshRoute style sheet · Layout, spacing and shape11

Lucide outlines, plus five of our own.

Apple's SF Symbols are licensed for Apple-platform apps only, so the web app uses Lucide (ISC licence), registered as Astryx theme icons. Its line icons sit close to SF Symbols' regular weight. Stroke 1.75 at 24 px, 2.25 at 32 px and up; rounded caps and joins. Icons always sit next to a word, apart from universal ones (close, back, more).

Hard brake

Forward arrow meeting a stop bar. Deceleration of 0.45 g or more.

Sharp turn

Turn arrow with a sway mark. Lateral force of 0.4 g or more.

Bump

Road bump with a lift arrow. Vertical spike of 0.5 g or more.

Cold timer

Snowflake and clock, kept apart so both read at 24 px.

Recording

Camera with a solid dot. The one Tomato that does not mean late.

The recording dot exception

A red recording dot is a universal convention and people look for it. It stays Tomato, but it's always a small dot with the word "REC" in Plex Mono, never a ring or a bar, so it can't be confused with the Late state.

Thresholds are starting points from published fleet-telematics practice and get tuned with real drives during user testing. No emoji anywhere in the interface.

FreshRoute style sheet · The map14

Promise vs. forecast, over the whole drive.

ETA accuracy · Van 2 · 19 Nov

Arrived 10:19 · promised 10:13 · +6 min
Forecast Promise ±3 min band Slipping / motion Cold loaded⋯ Recording gap

Rules

  • The y-axis is clock time, not "minutes late", so managers read it like a schedule.
  • The promise is a dashed Sprout line with a ±3 min band. The forecast line takes the colour of its state.
  • Annotations name the cause in plain words, taken from the driver's "what got in the way" answer.
  • Event ticks under the chart use the same track as the video scrubber, so the chart and the footage line up.
  • Every metric has a baseline: "+6 min vs. 9 min average on this route".
  • Axis and tick text: Plex Mono 11–12 px, Asphalt 400. No 3D, no pies, no gradient fills.
FreshRoute style sheet · Charts and the report timeline15

Lively when parked, still when moving.

You asked for lots of responsive animation, and the parked screens and the manager side get it. Once the van moves (GPS speed > 8 km/h), only motion that carries information is left: the ring filling, the puck moving, a state colour easing from one value to the next. Nothing slides, bounces or pulses for decoration.

Rules

  • Sentence case everywhere. Button labels are verbs that say exactly what happens.
  • Times are local clock times ("10:11"), plus a relative time only when it's under an hour.
  • Never blame the driver. Questions about delays offer causes (traffic, store not ready, train, loading took longer) and "something else".
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji, no "Oops".
  • Footage language is factual and neutral: "saved", "marked", "shared with North Loop Food Pantry".

One status vocabulary (Careit audit #04)

Scheduled En route to pickup Loaded En route to food bank Delivered

These exact words appear in the driver app, manager view, alerts, the store link and the report, with no synonyms.

FreshRoute style sheet · Voice and words18

One theme file, about 180 components.

Everything above becomes one local Astryx theme, freshroute, extending Neutral. Astryx components then pick up the colours, faces, radii and motion without per-screen styling. Glass and the map are the only custom layers.

  name: 'freshroute',
  extends: neutralTheme,
  palette: themePalette({ accent: '#B8F25E', neutral: '#66726D',
    success: '#B8F25E', warning: '#FFB54A',
    danger: '#FF7A66', info: '#7FD6F0' }),
  typography: {
    scale: { base: 17, ratio: 1.2 },
    body:    { family: '-apple-system', fallbacks: '"Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif' },
    heading: { family: 'Overpass', url: '/fonts/overpass.css' },
    code:    { family: 'IBM Plex Mono', url: '/fonts/plex-mono.css' },
  },
  radius: { base: 12, multiplier: 1 },
  motion: { tempo: 1, character: 'standard' },

Accent and success share Sprout on purpose: the primary action is the on-time path. Focus rings and selection use Asphalt 100, so focus never reads as a status. Decisions I'd like you to check.

#DecisionWhy, and the alternative
1Sprout lime as the one brand colour, which also means "on time"It ties the brand to the promise and stands out from the blue/purple of maps and Life360. The alternative is a deeper green (#4CC38A), which is calmer but closer to every food-rescue app.
2Daylight mode on driving screens during daytimeReadability in sun, as on page 07. If you want dark mode always, drivers can still pin it in Preferences.
3System font for UI instead of a web fontIt's native on Apple hardware and costs nothing to load. The trade-off is that Windows managers see Segoe UI. The alternative is Overpass for interface text too, which costs about 40 KB.
4Overpass as the display face (your pick)Road-sign heritage, legible at distance. Shown here on every headline; tell me if it reads too wide anywhere.
5Glass only on floating controlsApple's own rule for Liquid Glass, and it keeps reports readable. More glass would look more "Liquid Glass" but hurts contrast.
6Motion goes quiet above 8 km/hDriver safety. Parked and manager screens keep the full motion set.
7App icon: direction A, ring and leaf (your pick, page 13)The small cut (no notch, no vein, thicker ring) is used at 64 px and below.
8State thresholds: on time ≤ +3 min, slipping +4–10, late > +10; cold timer warns at 1 h and 2 hStarting values. The food bank can edit them, and user testing will tell us if they're right.
