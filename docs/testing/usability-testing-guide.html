1

The plan on one page

You're checking two things: can a volunteer plan and run a pickup run without help, and can someone at the pantry tell when food is arriving and check it in? Every session gives you the same four numbers per task, so sessions can be compared.

What you measure

  • Completed: finished without help
  • With help: finished after a hint
  • Gave up: stopped or ran out of time
  • Seconds from "Start task" to the outcome
  • SUS score (0 to 100) from the 10-question survey at the end
  • Notes: where they hesitated, and what they said, word for word

Who does what (rotate each session)

  • Facilitator: reads the script and tasks, runs the test panel, stays neutral.
  • Note-taker: writes down what happens and quotes. Says nothing.
  • Participant: uses the iPad and thinks out loud.
  • In the car only, the safety passenger: sits beside the driver and handles the iPad while the car moves.

Who to test with

Aim for 5 people per group. Five people usually surface most of the big problems; after that you mostly see the same ones again.

2

Before test day

  1. Book people. Ask Cody for 5 volunteers and 2 to 5 staff. Offer 45 minutes at the pantry. Ask each one: "Do you pick up or receive food for the pantry? How often? Do you use a phone or tablet for it now?" Write the answers down; they go next to the results.
  2. Pick one iPad for testing and charge it. Test results stay on that iPad until you export them, so don't switch iPads mid-day.
  3. Turn on test mode. Open FreshRoute from the Home Screen icon and press and hold the FreshRoute logo for a second (on Welcome, or top left on the pantry screens), then tap OK. Drivers can also use the Test mode switch in Preferences. The test panel appears and stays on, even after closing the app, until you turn it off the same way. Participants won't see the logo trick unless you show them.
  4. Allow the camera, microphone, location and motion once, yourself, so the participant isn't greeted by permission pop-ups. Do one full practice run: Welcome, Try the demo pantry, a simulated drive at 8×.
  5. Check the food bank delivery. In the demo, a new food bank truck comes every 2 hours. For about the first hour of each 2-hour block it's on the road; after that it's at the door. Pantry tasks P1 to P3 work either way, but the wording on screen changes, so note which state it was in.
  6. Print section 8: one checklist per day, one note sheet per participant.
  7. Decide where notes go, for example a shared folder named FreshRoute testing with one file per participant: P1-driver.pdf, and the CSV export at the end of the day.

Say this to everyone, every time

The stores are real, but their donation schedules and items in the app are made up for the prototype. Don't send anyone to a store's back door asking for a pickup. The food bank orders and truck are sample data too.

3

Running a session, step by step

  1. Reset the app. In the test panel: Log tab, then Reset app for next participant. It wipes the last person's setup, route and receipts but keeps the test log. The app goes back to Welcome.
  2. Set the participant name at the top of the test panel: P1, P2… No real names anywhere in the app or the export.
  3. Welcome and consent. Read:
    "Thanks for helping. We're testing the app, not you. There are no wrong answers, and if something is confusing, that's exactly what we need to find. Please say what you're thinking as you go, even small things like 'I'm looking for…'. I'll read you some tasks and mostly stay quiet. [Name] will take notes. Is it OK if we write down what you say? We won't use your name. You can stop at any time."
    If you want to record the session's screen or audio, ask separately, and don't record if they say no.
  4. Hand over the iPad on the screen the task starts from. Hide the test panel (the X) so it doesn't distract.
  5. Read the task out loud, exactly as written in the panel, then tap Start task. Don't explain or rephrase.
  6. Stay quiet. If they ask you something, answer with a question:
    "What would you try if I weren't here?" · "What are you looking for?" · "What do you expect that to do?"
    Give a hint only after about 2 minutes of being stuck, or if they're getting upset. A hint means the task is With help.
  7. End the task. Tap Completed, With help or Gave up. Type one line in the notes box first if something stood out. The panel moves to the next task.
  8. Survey. After the last task, open the Survey tab and hand them the iPad: 10 statements, 1 to 5. Don't comment on their answers.
  9. Wrap-up questions (note-taker writes the answers):
    • "What was the hardest part?"
    • "Was anything different from how you do this today?"
    • "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
    • Drivers: "How would you feel about recording video and sound on a drive?"
    • Pantry staff: "Would you trust the arrival time? What would make you trust it more?"
  10. Thank them, then take 15 minutes: tidy the notes, and reset the app before the next person.

Driver tasks

Start on the Welcome screen. For a session in a room, turn on Simulate the drive on the "Before you drive" screen and pick 8×: the van moves by itself, and the times it records (arrival, cold timer, report) are still realistic.

Tip

At 8×, the van covers the roads between stops in a minute or two, then waits at each store until the participant taps Loaded. The simulator panel on the navigation screen shows "Drive time": the app's clock for that drive, which runs 8 times faster than the real one.

6

The real drive, in a car

This is the one test the simulator can't replace: GPS, glare, a mount, a real turn you miss. Do it with your group before you do it with a participant.

Safety rules (not optional)

  • Nobody touches the iPad while the car is moving except the safety passenger. Texas bans reading or typing on a phone while driving; treat the iPad the same way.
  • Set up the route and do every task while parked. While moving, the driver only glances at the next turn, the way they would with any navigation app.
  • The iPad is in a mount, not on a lap or seat. Daylight, a route you know, no highway for the first run.
  • The app records video and sound once the drive starts: tell everyone in the car before you start.
  • Park in the store lot for the pickup step. Don't go to the receiving door (the schedules are made up).

Before you leave

  • Check your iPad model. A Wi-Fi-only iPad has no GPS, so location drops once you leave Wi-Fi. Use a cellular iPad, or run the app on an iPhone for this test.
  • Plug the iPad into the car. Turn on Do Not Disturb.
  • Open FreshRoute from the Home Screen icon and plan 2 stops close together, for example Kroger on University Dr and ALDI on Ector St (both demo stores are always open).
  • On "Before you drive", leave Simulate the drive off, check location, allow motion, pick the cameras.

On the road, the safety passenger notes

  • Did the next-turn instruction come early enough? Was the distance readable at a glance?
  • Any missed turn: did a new route appear within about 15 seconds?
  • Did the arrival screen open by itself at each store? (Stores have a 70-metre arrival zone; big lots might need "I've arrived".)
  • Glare, text size and brightness in daylight. Did the screen ever turn off?
  • Anything the driver said out loud.

After

Finish the drive at the pantry and open the report together. Compare the report's drive time and arrival times with what you remember. Check the video has sound and that the hard-brake or bump markers match real moments.

7

After the sessions: scoring and fixing

  1. Export. Test panel, Log tab, Export CSV. One file has every task row and every SUS score. Save it to the shared folder the same day.
  2. Count. For each task, out of everyone who tried it: how many Completed, With help, Gave up; and the middle (median) time.
  3. Read the SUS scores. The panel already does the maths. Around 68 is average for software; above 80 is good; below 50 means people struggled.
  4. Sort the notes. Write each problem on its own sticky note (one problem, one participant). Group the ones that are the same problem. Count how many people hit each one.
  5. Rate each problem on this scale, then fix from the top:
    0Not a usability problem
    1Cosmetic: fix if there's time
    2Minor: slows people down
    3Major: people needed help; fix before the next round
    4Blocks the task: fix first
  6. Write it up in this order: what you tested and with whom; the results table; the top problems with a quote each; what you'll change. One quote beats three sentences of summary.
  7. Test again after fixing, with new people, so you can see whether the numbers moved.

8

Day-of checklist

  • iPad charged, charger and stand in the bag
  • FreshRoute open from the Home Screen icon, test mode on (hold the logo), test panel visible
  • Camera, microphone, location and motion already allowed
  • One full practice run done today
  • Note sheets printed, one per participant, and pens
  • Roles decided: facilitator, note-taker
  • For a car test: mount, car charger, safety passenger, cellular iPad or iPhone

Between participants

  • Log tab, Reset app for next participant
  • New participant name in the panel (P2, P3…)
  • Notes from the last session written up

End of day

  • Export CSV and save it to the shared folder
  • Note sheets photographed or scanned
  • Top 3 problems written down while they're fresh
