Test FreshRoute
on your own

For the four of us. Each person tests the app alone, on their own time, with their own phone or iPad and their own car. Nobody else to schedule. About 2 hours each, then one group meeting to put it together.

1

How this works

Each of us plays all three users: the volunteer who drives pickups, the person at the pantry waiting for food, and the food bank's truck driver bringing it. You time yourself with the test panel built into the app, talk out loud while a recording runs, and write down every problem you hit. At the end we compare notes.

What each person hands in

  • Your results file, named with your initials, for example SM-results.csv
  • Your problem log (page 9), filled in
  • Your screen recording from Part A, if you made one
  • The report link from your Part B drive (the app keeps the video and sound)

Put them in one shared folder, for example FreshRoute testing, before the group meeting.

Be honest about what this test is

We know the app, so we'll breeze past things a newcomer would get stuck on. This round is best at catching bugs, confusing words and anything that breaks on a real road. To make it fairer: write down your first reaction to every screen, even if you've seen it before; don't look anything up in the code or docs mid-task; and count anything you had to guess as "with help". A round with real pantry volunteers can come later.

Split the order

Two of us start Part A with the pantry tasks, two start with the driver tasks. Whatever you do first, you learn from, so splitting the order keeps that from skewing every result the same way.

2

Set up your device

  1. Pick your device. For the drive you need GPS: an iPhone, or an iPad with cellular. A Wi-Fi-only iPad has no GPS and loses its location once you leave Wi-Fi. It's fine for Part A, but use your phone for Part B. (FreshRoute is designed for iPad; on a phone, note anything that looks cramped or cut off as a problem.)
  2. Add it to your Home Screen. In Safari, open freshroute.pages.dev, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, with Open as Web App on. From now on, open FreshRoute from that icon.
  3. Turn on test mode. On the Welcome screen, press and hold the FreshRoute logo for a second and tap OK. A testing panel appears in the corner. It stays on, even after you close the app, until you hold the logo again.
  4. Add yourself. Tap Try the demo pantry, then "I'm not on the list", and enter your name. Everyone on the demo pantry sees the list, so next time you just tap your name. The test panel uses your name as the participant, and "Just mine" on the pantry screens shows only what you did.
  5. Allow everything once. Camera, microphone, location and motion. If you said no to one by accident: Settings, Apps, Safari (or Privacy & Security, Location Services, for location).
  6. Optional, but worth it: record your screen during Part A. Open Control Center, long-press the record button, turn the microphone on, and talk out loud as you go. Watching it back is where most of the good notes come from.

How to use the test panel on yourself

  1. Pick the task in the panel and read it once.
  2. Tap Start task, then hide the panel with the X so it isn't in your way.
  3. Do the task. Say what you're looking for and what you expect, out loud.
  4. Open the panel again (flask button) and pick an outcome: Completed (no trouble), With help (you had to guess, retry, or remember how it works), Gave up. Add one line of notes.

Before Part A, use the Log tab's Reset app for next participant once, so you start from a clean Welcome screen. It keeps your results.

Part A2: the delivery loop

This follows one order from the food bank to the pantry door, and you play both ends. The app saves the delivery on the server, so it carries over when you switch roles.

Two devices make this easier

If you have a phone and an iPad, be the pantry on one and the food bank driver on the other, with test mode on both. With one device: do L1 and L2, then Reset app for next participant (Log tab), join as the food bank driver for L3 to L6, reset again, and join as the pantry for L7. Watch for

Does typing the invoice lines make sense, and did the frozen item get marked cold? With two devices, glance at the pantry side during L4 to L6: does the card update live and the truck move on the map? Does anything on the food bank driver's screens still talk about "pickups" or "stores"?

The demo pantry is shared by all four of us: remove any of your orders you didn't receive (Remove, on the card).

5

Part B: your drive

This is the part the simulator can't fake: real GPS, sun on the screen, a turn you miss, a parking lot bigger than the app expects. You're alone, so the app's own recording is your note-taker: it records video and sound once the drive starts, so talk out loud.

Safety rules (not optional)

  • Don't touch the screen while the car is moving. Set everything up while parked. While driving, only glance at the next turn, like any navigation app. Texas bans reading or typing on a phone while driving; treat the app the same way.
  • If you need to tap anything, pull over and park first. Missed a turn? Keep driving; the app plans a new route by itself.
  • Phone or iPad in a mount, plugged in. Never on your lap or the seat.
  • Daylight, dry weather, streets you know, no highways. If anyone rides along, tell them sound is recorded.
  • The stores are real but their donation schedules in the app are made up. Park in the lot; don't go to the receiving door or talk to staff about a pickup.

Before you start (parked)

  1. Start near the pantry pin (the house pin, around East McKinney St in Denton), in a public lot. The route and promised time start there. The pantry is made up, so don't park at its address.
  2. Plan 2 nearby stores from the always-open demo stores: Kroger on W University Dr, Walmart Supercenter on S Loop 288, ALDI on Ector St, Sprouts on Teasley Ln, or Natural Grocers on E Sherman Dr. Two close together keeps it to about half an hour.
  3. On "Before you drive": Simulate the drive off, check location, allow motion, pick your cameras, sound on. Tap Start drive and say the promised time out loud, so it's on the recording.

While driving, notice (say it out loud)

Does each turn come early enough, and can you read the distance in a glance? If you leave the route, does a new one appear within about 15 seconds? Is the screen readable in sun, and does it ever dim? Does the arrival time move in ways that make sense?

At each store (parked)

The pickup screen should open by itself in the lot. If not, tap "I've arrived" and note it (big lots can be outside the 70-metre arrival zone). Enter counts, tap Loaded, drive on.

At the end (parked)

Finish the drive near the pantry pin. Check the report against what really happened: times, the cold timer, and the video's sound and bump markers. Copy its link for the folder.

6

Part C: write it up

  1. Do the survey. Test panel, Survey tab: 10 statements, 1 to 5. Answer for the whole app, both parts. The panel works out your score (0 to 100).
  2. Export. Log tab, Export CSV. Rename the file with your initials (SM-results.csv) and put it in the shared folder.
  3. Watch your recordings at 1.5× speed: the Part A screen recording and the Part B drive video. Every time you hesitated, said "huh", or did something twice, that's a problem for the log.
  4. Fill in the problem log (page 9). One row per problem: where it happened, what happened, how bad. Use the scale below.
  5. Mark your top 3: the problems you'd fix first if you could only fix three.

How bad is it?

ScoreMeaning
1Cosmetic: looks off, didn't slow you down
2Minor: slowed you down, you worked it out
3Major: you had to guess, retry or remember how it works
4Blocker: you couldn't finish, or it was unsafe on the road

Good problem notes

Where: Receive delivery. What happened: I scrolled past Gala apples twice because the list isn't in the same order as the paper invoice. Score: 3.
Not: "receiving was confusing."

7

The group meeting

Once all four folders are in. One person shares their screen and keeps the merged list; everyone else reads out their log.

  1. Results table (10 min). Open the four CSV files. For each task, count how many of us got Completed, With help or Gave up, and the middle time. Note the four survey scores (around 68 is average for software; above 80 is good).
  2. Merge problems (20 min). Read problems out one at a time. If someone else hit the same thing, merge them and note how many of us hit it. Keep the highest score anyone gave it.
  3. Rank (10 min). Sort by score, then by how many of us hit it. Anything rated 4, and anything unsafe on the road, goes first no matter what.
  4. Decide (5 min). Pick what gets fixed before the next round, and who checks each fix. Write down what we're not fixing and why.
